Part C — Synthesis: from the arts to gameplay PROPOSAL

Everything in this part is a PROPOSAL built on the RESEARCH above. Numeric thresholds are untested starting guesses, not facts. Neutral names only. No show names or moves. Third-party Avatar/Korra material is research input, not WIR canon.

C1. What the research says, in plain words

  1. Every art teaches the same shape of movement: root first, then waist, then arms, with the whole body connected (videos #2, #4, #6, #7). That is a ground-up chain, and it backs the stage-order checker.
  2. Two chain modes exist (video #7): sequential (feet → hips → torso → hands, for a stomp or sweep) and together (hips and shoulders in the same phase, for slow flow). Our checker should support both.
  3. Teaching comes in three stages (video #6): HOLD a shape, LINK it into one smooth motion, then (optionally) SNAP for speed. Beginners only need HOLD. Keep SNAP out of the early game.
  4. Breath and stillness count as skill (videos #4, #5): count the breath, reward the hold.
  5. The cleanest software model of an ordered, timed gesture is Meta's Sequence pattern (#21): ordered steps, a min hold per step, a max time per step, cooldown, hysteresis, and a debug view.
  6. The most reliable camera engineering (#11 IRLSurfer, #13 Sadball): measure in shoulder-widths, smooth with a One Euro filter, require position and speed together, use a dead-band (hysteresis), calibrate for 2 seconds, show "STEP BACK" prompts.
  7. Biggest real-world failure (#2 Medium, #11): wrong distance and not enough space. Space/distance gating is the first feature, not the last.
  8. Hide-behind needs a person mask (#19): draw the effect first, then draw camera pixels where "person" is true.

C2. The stage-order checker — how it maps to a move PROPOSAL

For each move the checker watches a list of stages, each with: what signal, min hold, max step time, order.

Stage Body signal (MediaPipe landmark idea) Rule
0. Gate shoulder-width in frame, body fully visible, space clear must pass before anything else
1. Root ankle/knee: stance depth or foot-speed spike starts the chain
2. Hips hip-centre drop, push or turn peaks after root
3. Torso shoulder-line angle or lean peaks after hips (allowed gap ≈ 100–300 ms, untested)
4. Arms wrist velocity or span peaks after torso
5. Settle all velocities back under a calm threshold required to score the move
  • Score = order correct + timing within window + calm settle. Never score force.
  • Use a tolerance of about ±150 ms (the window IRLSurfer used) and a per-move cooldown.
  • Beginner level: HOLD stages only (1–2, plus settle). Level 2: LINK (all stages, slow). Level 3: optional faster timing windows.
  • Seated and hands-only variants use only stages 3–5.
  • A "debug tree" (visible in a dev mode) shows which stage is firing, so Coach can tune moves by eye.

C4. Safety and ethics (applies to every mechanic) PROPOSAL

  • "Don't get smart to hurt nobody." The game targets are objects only: rocks, ribbons, rings, light. No person, avatar or other player is ever a target. No hit-detection against people.
  • No strikes, kicks, punches, joint locks or any fighting move as gameplay, even though several researched sources use them (#1, #7 in Part A, #22, #23, and some video content). We take only stance, breath, root, flow and timing.
  • Foot-speed spikes are used only for a stomp onto the floor, never a kick.
  • No jumps. No fast spinning. Slow tempo is rewarded; speed is never required at Level 1–2.
  • Clear-space prompt before every session; a rest pose that pauses the game; seated and hands-only options; left-handed/mirror option (#24).
  • Score is a game score only. It is not training data, a fitness test or a skill claim.
  • Privacy: process on-device, send no video (as in #16).
  • Canon: all of this is a PROPOSAL until a Canon Bot or RB1 lock. Nothing here is canon.
